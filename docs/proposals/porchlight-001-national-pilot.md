# PORCHLIGHT-001 — City + Outlier Field Pilot

**Proposal date:** 2026-10-27  
**Stage:** PILOT DESIGN / PARTNER DISCOVERY — **NOT AN ACTIVE SHELTER OR MATCHING SERVICE**  
**Owning experiment:** The Static Collective / NanaSpork Garden. No partner, host, volunteer, or placement is represented as enrolled or approved.

## Thesis

People experiencing homelessness should not have to knock on a hundred doors to discover which one is open. Build a **locally governed, nationally reusable hospitality and practical-support coordination layer**, with a route to stable housing rather than serial temporary placements.

**Rotate the helpers, not the person who needs rest.** A constantly rotating backyard tent is neither a safe nor a lawful default. Backyard camping, vehicle stays and micro-shelters are jurisdiction- and property-specific; no such placement is offered through this pilot without documented permission and adequate facilities. Severe weather, accessibility and sanitation must be accounted for.

This complements—not replaces—affordable housing, case management, emergency shelter, domestic-violence services, and formal coordinated entry.

## Evidence and national targeting

The newest **published national** HUD PIT dataset as of this proposal is the **January 2025** enumeration, released in **May 2026**: **745,652** counted experiencing homelessness, **266,320** unsheltered. PIT estimates are one-night, method-dependent and undercount people not observed, including many staying temporarily with others. CoC areas are *not always municipal boundaries*.

There are two different targets. **High-volume metros** are where the greatest number of people are counted; **rate outliers** can expose acute need in lower-population places. We deliberately test both instead of allowing total counts alone to allocate attention.

**Work queues, not simultaneous launches:**

- **Field rehearsal — Waseca, MN:** test nonresidential offer/need/receipt handling with local consenting adults and service professionals, if available. No unsupported overnight placements.
- **Wave A target — Los Angeles + Humboldt:** one large metro and one rural outlier; partner discovery and legal/safety review before any field placements.
- **Wave B watchlist — Seattle/King + Central Oregon:** partner reconnaissance; no enrollments or service claims.
- **Adaptation tracks — NYC + Portland + Monroe County:** connect to existing host programs, established referral infrastructures and local climate constraints.

## Distinct offerings

1. **Neighbor support**: food, laundry, phone charging, transportation, documents assistance, storage handled by an approved partner, and other bounded acts. Can pilot first; no living-space exposure required.
2. **Safe indoor respite**: spare-room or existing authorized space for a stable, agreed period, only through a qualified local operator following property, insurance, legal, screening, grievance and safeguarding checks. Not daily forced moves.
3. **Housing bridge**: link with housing navigators/coordinated entry and document a long-term housing plan where the guest wants one. Reconnection with friends/family only if freely chosen and safe.
4. **Future supervised sites**: research municipally authorized micro-shelters or safe parking separately. Not live; no workaround for zoning or weather restrictions.

## Local authority, minimum safeguards and go/no-go gates

No live guest–host introductions and no promises of emergency refuge until **all** locally applicable gates are satisfied:

- A named **local operating organization** and **accountable program lead** accept responsibility; written partnership scope.
- At least one **person with lived experience of homelessness** has independently reviewed participation, consent, burden and grievance handling; compensate when possible.
- Written check of **zoning, occupancy, landlord/HOA requirements, insurance, tenancy and habitability rules, accessibility, fire safety, sanitation**, and extreme-weather response for any residential offer.
- Host identity/reference/background process and training approved by the operator; **guests are not publicly rated, scored, or coerced into disclosure**.
- A private, reachable **safeguarding contact**, independent complaint channel, incident/mandatory-reporting protocol, and documented plan for failed or withdrawn placements.
- Opt-in, revocable **guest consent**; housing assistance cannot be conditioned on religious practice, labor, publicity, personal storytelling, or platform participation.
- Documented integration with **CoC/coordinated entry** and existing emergency routes; this network is not a substitute for immediate crisis care.
- Privacy/data-retention review, access controls, and storage minimized to the smallest proof necessary. No public guest names, health histories, exact sites, host addresses, or refuge locations.
- **Only adult-focused nonresidential support in the first field experiment.** Youth/minor placements and high-risk domestic-violence sheltering require an already qualified specialist program and separate safeguarding.

If any gate fails: HOLD, narrow to safe nonresidential assistance or STOP. No silent exception.

## Thirty-day gated experiment (targets, not claims of field activity)

**Days 1–7: discovery and safety.** Seek permission for conversations with CoC leaders in LA and Humboldt; recruit an independent lived-experience reviewer; inventory official resources and obtain local legal/safety consultation. Draft a data-sharing agreement. Conduct Waseca test with *synthetic* needs first.

**Days 8–14: opt-in capacity inventory.** Aim for 10 *prospective* community contributors per trial geography, recording only generalized resource category and contact preference in a properly secured partner channel. Avoid publication or automatic matching. Identify which assistance categories partners actually need.

**Days 15–21: supervised nonresidential trial.** Subject to named operator and consent, route 3–5 concrete assistance requests per operating geography with human review. A referral is not fulfillment. Each completed action needs receiver confirmation, and each refusal or inability to help remains visible to authorized local coordinators.

**Days 22–30: independent outcome review.** Target a minimum of five confirmed nonresidential acts across the trial, subject to approvals. Review delays, dignity/choice, adverse events, unmet needs, volunteer burden, and path to housing. Residential pilot activation is a *separate* explicit gate; no stay happens merely because a 30-day clock expired.

**Decision after 30 days:** expand / revise / stop. Publish only aggregate, non-identifying outcomes with source provenance and explicit limitations.

## Metrics and evidence boundary

Track: offered capacity; verified and approved capacity; time to human triage; referral acceptances/refusals; **physically confirmed** completed help acts; guest-reported dignity and safety (voluntary); unresolved needs; housing navigator linkages; safety incidents; host/steward burnout. For any future housing placement also track consecutive safe nights without forced moves, and 7-/30-day housing follow-up where consented.

Do **not** equate an offer with an available bed; a booked night with stable housing; an automated receipt with a human-confirmed completed act; a code deployment with field proof; or a participant's absence from follow-up with success.

## Proposed reLATTE + NanaSpork boundary

`offer.drafted → local steward HOLD → partner verifies eligibility/scope → human review → referral proposed → participant consent → partner accepts/refuses → act reported → receiver confirms → follow-up`

Receipts transport *evidence of process*, not a guest's biography. ReLATTE carries signed references across independently governed operators; each recipient retains HOLD/ADMIT/REFUSE. NanaSpork/Garden projects only properly authorized Help Slip need/offer events. No autonomous matchmaking, reputation, public routing, or cross-region transfer of shelter addresses. Existing Garden functions are **not asserted to satisfy production safeguarding**.
